Number labels follow the exchange's four-way rule: 本片實例 (single-project instance), 可沿用做法 (portable practice), 外部做法 (external practice, not tested by us), 外部規格 (external spec, with source and check date, must be followed at delivery).

## 5.1 Process and gates

1. Split G1 into G1a (plan approval) and G1b (spend authorisation); the two never substitute for each other. Platform routes state a usage-percentage range and are checked against the stop line at the range's upper bound, which must include failed or interrupted batches; if the upper bound crosses the stop line, report before starting. Money routes state amount, currency and stop line. The 85% figure is a single-project instance (convention default, not measured). (D-M178)
2. G1b states a money cap; at 80% cumulative spend, stop and report with remaining shots, estimated remaining cost and a continue-or-stop recommendation. Platform quota is managed like money. (D-M187)
3. A method change needs fresh approval: changing model, vendor, generation route, resolution or length all count. A re-roll with the same model and settings is not a method change, but its cost still counts toward the G1b cap. A money approval does not cover a method change. (D-M187)
4. Every rule states which film types it applies to; before carrying a rule across film types, check the do-not-carry list. This risk is preventive; no actual mis-carry is on record. (D-M179)
5. Zero-cost recalibration may proceed without separate approval when it generates nothing, spends no quota and takes none of the reviewer's time; touching any of those sends it back to G1a or G1b. (D-M180)
6. Label every number one of four ways: single-project instance (named project and context, example only); portable practice (state how a new film measures and recomputes it); external practice (labelled "external, not tested by us", frame of reference only); external spec (source and check date, must be followed at delivery). Ratios and scores are check signals, not pass targets. (D-M162, finalising D-M124)
7. Disagreements are argued on evidence until they converge; they are not escalated to the owner. Every item states a recommended practice and the reasons for and against; evidence ranks measured above literature above convention. Where evidence cannot decide, take the low-cost reversible option, label it "convention, untested", and keep the verification or test open. (D-M88, D-M122)
8. Every item carries an explicit position with reasons: argue for what the evidence supports, and when persuaded say so plainly and change the wording. No splitting the difference. (D-M123)
9. After full convergence, the editor assembles the final list with dialogue IDs; each side writes it into its own Skill and SOP and reads back to confirm; neither side edits the other's files. the owner is told the write-in result and how any open item converged, not asked to rule. (D-M122, D-M169)
10. Gates follow risk, not runtime: grade by four risk factors, keep a must-protect list and evidence items; a slim set bible is for single episodes only. (D-G01, D-G17)

## 5.2 Casting and likeness QC

11. Use a three-link likeness baseline chain: photo ceiling, approved character-sheet target, impostor-group floor. Record both baselines, not a single photo score. (D-M92, D-M98, D-M142)
12. The impostor group uses only other real people in the same project who consented to take part, plus synthetic faces from the same pipeline. Never borrow real faces from other projects, even with consent confirmed. If same-gender faces are short, fill with same-pipeline synthetic faces through G1b; if no quota is to be spent, label "no same-gender impostor group, ratio threshold unusable" and judge by eye. (D-M175, correcting D-M92)
13. Thresholds are relative to the person's own baseline, computed on scene shots and graded by angle. Below the impostor floor is an automatic fail with no waiver. Recompute the floor per pipeline; 0.30 is a single-project instance (Humangentic v7 pipeline). (D-M188)
14. Changing any of the image model, video model or scoring model means recomputing the impostor distribution and floor from scene shots of the new pipeline: re-run the distribution over all frames, take each face's highest impostor score, state the face-width floor, and set the floor with headroom above it. (D-M201)
15. Between floor and target, the human eye decides. Waiver records use 8 columns (the original 7 including mandatory pipeline version, plus a waiver-reason column); reasons come only from a fixed category list (angle, expression, lighting, small face pixels, styling target) plus one human-judgement sentence. (D-M193)
16. Rejection records use the same fixed 8 columns including mandatory pipeline version and the fixed-category waiver reason; calibrate only once enough samples exist, never set thresholds from a handful of samples. (D-M193)
17. The exception table has 10 mandatory columns in four classes; unmeasurable values are filled with "none", never left blank. Class 1 (no face detected) records score, face width and yaw as "none". "Not detected" and "below standard" are counted separately. 0.55 / 0.45 and the face-width examples are uncalibrated single-project instances, replaced per film after calibration. (D-M166)
18. Calibration uses only new fixed-column records that include pipeline version; impostor recomputation is zero-cost and goes first. The +0.05 margin over the highest impostor score and the figures 0.409 / 0.310 / 0.388 / 0.273 are single-project instances (Automation Video). (D-M180)
19. Whether the scene baseline sample is sufficient is judged at shot level: resample whole shots and check whether p10 crosses the highest impostor score; if it crosses, the sample is insufficient and scoring switches to an angle-independent threshold plus human judgement. "15 scene faces per person per angle and at least 3 shots" is a single-project instance (experience value, no statistical test). The earlier fixed trigger "at least 30 shots, at least 5 rejected" is withdrawn. (D-M175, D-M125, D-M139)
20. At the sheet stage, check photo quality first; if it is too poor, replace the photo before anything else. A low ceiling caused by photo quality is not a per-shot failure. (D-M175, D-M181)
21. Likeness records must note the circularity limit when attribution and scoring share a source. Scores and photos stay in the project folder; shared tables carry statistics only. (D-M175, D-M194)
22. Multi-person shots do not lower the score threshold: a same-frame drop is judged a generation failure and the staging is changed first, splitting to 2–3 people per frame so faces get larger, then re-measuring per person. Anyone still low after enlargement is handled individually (new reference photo or a solo shot). Systematic multi-person low scores mean re-staging, not a lower bar. (D-M201)
23. The face-width floor is provisionally 90 px (labelled convention, uncalibrated, single-project instance). Below it, label "detection interference" and judge by eye; it counts as neither pass nor fail. No cut line is set for cartoon co-stars in long shots. (D-M96, D-M139)
24. Keep a model appendix table with version and last-verified date; old rows are marked expired, never deleted. Before scoring, score every face against every cast member, then assign identities one-to-one, with a human confirming every identity by eye; frames with missed detections are not scored. One-to-one assignment is stated as actually used, not benchmarked against other matching methods. (D-M189)
25. Synthetic characters are not excluded up front: a synthetic face must score below the impostor floor against every real cast member and is recorded as an interference class. (D-M189)
26. Any quoted face score must state its source and measurement tool, the comparison baseline, still or video, face width and angle. 0.412 (SH28 best static keyframe, face width 131 px) and 0.584 (Ep4 series-lead best keyframe, face width 230 px) come from FINAL_FIX_LIST E5-SH28, insightface buffalo_l against the 05_qc anchors; single-project instances, never quote across projects. (D-M186)
27. Exception outcomes are named uniformly WAIVE / WAIVE-near / FAIL-KEPT, alongside the four-class exception statistics. (D-G20, D-G24)
28. Never calibrate new thresholds on old judgements as if they were ground truth: old records are reference only, otherwise the calibration is circular. All three sides' original thresholds were convention, uncalibrated. (D-047)

## 5.3 Generation and prompts

29. The script side writes intent plus reasons, with numbers marked as proposals; the production side writes numbers plus verification. Focal lengths and distances in a script are starting proposals, not fixed values. (D-M83)
30. Label M-26 in two layers: the optical principle is grounded, its effect on the generator is untested. Do not file it as plain "convention" before it is tested. (D-M176)
31. In prompts, write camera distance and focal length as separate variables, never bound into one number. (D-M176)
32. Fold the focal-length / distance A/B into the next real-person project as a pilot: first frames only, about 1 m and about 2 m crossed with two focal lengths, 3 seeds per cell (a portable-practice proposal starting point, untested; actual distances and focal lengths follow that film's shot table). Compare scene-shot ArcFace medians plus human judgement of perspective. Spend goes through G1b first; resulting score differences are labelled single-project instances. (D-M176)
33. TECH-39 is kept only as a command and vocabulary reference to help think of available techniques; its 100 item numbers are not adopted as a shared language. Formal records always write technique name (Chinese and English) plus tool plus rule plus our own check-item ID, never an external number. The library intake record notes "transcribed by the editor, not verified item by item by the Grok team", evidence grade "practitioner community cheat sheet", with post link and read date. (D-M213, D-M214)

## 5.4 Editing and freeze frames

34. Freeze frames fall into four classes: (1) designed freeze; (2) a screen or prop that is static by nature; (3) a brief static tail after an action completes, tolerable; (4) freezing mid-action on the main line to hide a generation flaw, which is a quiet downgrade. Only class 4 counts toward the trigger criteria; a script-designed freeze is not a downgrade. (D-M173)
35. Freeze judgement turns on narrative function and whether the main-line action is sacrificed, never on seconds alone; there is no universal percentage threshold. The 5% trigger value is withdrawn. Per-episode freeze ratios are single-project instance records and check reminders only. (D-M172, D-M135)
36. Prevent class 4 at the source with A+B keyframes or re-staging, not by freezing in the edit. A mid-action freeze must be regenerated or prevented at source; a static tail is tolerated only briefly and only after the action completes. (D-M173, D-M112, D-M121)
37. Log every freeze segment in the EDL with its class, in-shot start and end seconds and reason, and mirror it in the FIX_LIST. From now on freezes are marked in the EDL, never reconstructed from memory. (D-M173)
38. Freeze measurement: align with ECC affine first to cancel push-ins and pans, then read the residual; 0.75 s or more of near-zero residual is listed as a freeze, cross-checked against the fix record as a second source. Report a three-threshold range and use the middle threshold as the formal figure; a human then classifies each segment. 0.75 s and the series' three-threshold set are single-project instances. (D-M167)
39. Recalibrate freeze thresholds per film against that film's known still segments and known quiet real-action shots; never carry another film's threshold numbers over. (D-M167, D-M173)
40. Route freezes by source: a static screen means animating the UI first, a frozen person goes through the regeneration criteria, a designed freeze is not a problem. (D-M179)
41. SH28 stays at v1_7 and goes to the season-end fix list; it is not handled in the current version round. (D-M186)
42. Freeze and black-frame checks use the FFmpeg freezedetect and blackdetect of §2.12; parameters are starting defaults, recalibrated per film against known intentional holds and known flaw segments, with adjusted values and reasons written into that film's QC record. Check items prefer verifiable standard tools and official documentation, evidence grade "official documentation; external practices not benchmarked in this table". (D-M214, D-M213)
43. Cut detection currently relies on frame-difference spikes. PySceneDetect is an external, untested practice and is not listed as current practice. (D-M214)

## 5.5 Voice and sound

44. Q-143 addition: for polyphonic or error-prone characters, verify tone by aligning the pitch curve against a control character; judge brand names by phoneme recognition, never by ASR transcript text. Conclusions cover only the characters actually tested. (D-M192)
45. Chinese voice test protocol (finalised): candidate characters are checked against cross-strait pronunciation sources side by side (中華語文知識庫《兩岸常用詞典》 or Ministry of Education dictionaries against mainland《現代漢語詞典》); test characters come only from that film's actual script, and if the script yields fewer than 5 characters, test all of them and note the small sample. Decision rules are written in advance (per character, record whether the pitch curve lets the tone be read and whether the reading matches the expected pronunciation; a single misjudged character keeps the character-selection principle out of the supplement and records it as a known limit instead). (D-M192)
46. The extension principle "prefer characters whose cross-strait pronunciations differ" is decided only after that test runs. The test has not been run; tracking: run it on the next Chinese-voiced film using that film's own script and report back, spending no separate dubbing quota. (D-M192, D-M200)
47. Blind A/B listening and CER are recorded as not-done gaps, never written up as completed; to be filled at the next TTS pilot. (D-M192)
48. Put a music-route decision point in the planning stage: use, vocal needs, budget and licensing source. Before any external release, check the terms and keep evidence; never claim licensing is clear without that check. (D-M190)
49. Keep stock-library evidence in two places: the full terms text (preferred) or a screenshot in that version's music or QC folder on Drive; the PLAN records track name, source, licence name, permitted release types and check date with the Drive link. If a page needs login and its text cannot be captured, use a screenshot. (D-M191)
50. If the release type changes, recheck the licence terms. For uses the terms do not name, ask the licensor or change the track; never infer permission. (D-M191)
51. Backfill evidence only for the current final version: Humangentic v7c was backfilled (Mixkit "Pop Track 03", Mixkit Stock Music Free License, marked as checked after the fact, not at production time); the never-to-be-released v6c to v6i are not backfilled. (D-M190)
52. Read licence scope item by item: the v7c licence does not cover TV or broadcast, CD/DVD or games; going to TV means a new track or a separate licence. The reading that "time-stretching and cutting inside a film are permitted modifications" under the remix restriction is one side's interpretation, not legally confirmed, and is not stated as settled. (D-M190, D-M144)
53. If a BBC sound effect might be used publicly or on social platforms, replace it or buy a commercial licence at the PLAN stage; do not leave it until before release. (D-M94, D-M78)
54. Q-152: score tone correctness and accent separately; never merge them into one number. (D-G12, D-G22)

## 5.6 Subtitles and cards

55. Subtitle inner and outer frames are both hard caps and both must pass; in practice the stricter inner frame is the operating basis. The inner frame is a script-side formula (0.2 s per character plus 0.6 s; single-project instance, custom and untested, recomputed per film by audience age and on-screen competition). The outer frame is the Netflix Traditional Chinese norm (at most 16 characters per line; external practice, untested). (D-M184, D-M138)
56. Pass lines by audience: at most 7 characters per second for child audiences or when on-screen text competes, at most 9 per second otherwise; 9 per second is the outer frame's absolute cap, not an operating target. The numbers are from the Netflix norm and are untested by us. (D-M184, D-M138)
57. When the inner frame fails, fix in this order: cut or rewrite characters, lengthen the on-screen hold, split into two cards. Never loosen the cap. (D-M184)
58. Put redundant-subtitle questions into the model blind test: the model must state the on-screen basis for its answer; only when both runs flag the same card does it enter the delete-card self-check, followed by a blind-test re-run. It is a misread list only, never scored. (D-M184)
59. Card self-check belongs to the script side: when writing or revising an episode, check every card with "is it still understood without this text", and record deletions and rewrites in the project README decision log and the shot-table card column with a version bump. The production side shoots as written and collects new versions by numbered Q&A; when a blind test shows a deletion broke comprehension, it reports by numbered question, and restoring text or changing the picture is the script side's decision. Production never deletes cards on its own. (D-M185)
60. To read content, use a full-frame insert; to read relationships, use a tracked composite. At PLAN stage, measure the smallest text that must be read at delivery resolution, after warp: measure character height, contrast and sharpness together, take the blurriest frame of a moving screen, count hold time separately, and use the same film's readable and unreadable text as controls. (D-M177)
61. Screen text-height numbers are a single instance only: V5 B8 backfill (Chinese ink height unreadable at 8–9 px, marginal at 9–11 px, readable at 12–14 px, clear at 17–18 px; the same 9 px can split readable or not by contrast, sharpness and camera movement) is an Automation Video instance and is not a general threshold until more projects accumulate. (D-M177)
62. Storyboards pre-mark high-risk shots and how each person is recognised (face, costume, action, position); the script side owns correcting a wrong mark, the production side judges feasibility, and when generation cannot carry it, production reports by numbered question and the script side changes the recognition method or splits to a solo shot. Recognition by back view, costume or position applies per project rule, case by case, not as a general rule. (D-M186)

## 5.7 Delivery, blind testing and archiving

63. The three-layer blind test is best practice: layer 1 model blind test, layer 2 machine measurement, layer 3 the real-audience layer. There is no separate stranger-viewer gate; actual audience reaction is final calibration only. (D-M138, D-M169)
64. Run the model blind test twice per version and gate on the two misread lists: a version passes only when both runs misread none of the core events, character relationships and ending; when the two runs disagree, run a third and list the divergent items. Scores are recorded as trend only, never as thresholds; likeness never enters the blind test; the review sheet states whether the reviewer was a model or a human. (D-M165)
65. The machine-measurement layer owns measurable items such as subtitles and covers the model blind test's blind spots; likeness is handled by the likeness chain and never mixed into a blind-test score. (D-M138, D-M33, D-M91)
66. The real-audience layer is the actual audience, at most once before delivery: family films are reviewed by the owner, with family premiere reactions feeding only the next episode or the season-end fix list; client real-person films are watched by reviewers who know the person, for likeness and propriety; product films are watched once by one person who took no part in production (headcount rules in item 68). (D-M138)
67. Layer 3 for real-person films applies to real-person films only: someone who knows the person watches likeness and propriety. It does not extend to every film type. (D-M193)
68. Actual-audience calibration means that film's target audience member plus the owner's review: for a family series it is that series' subject, for a birthday film it is the birthday child. The target audience's reaction is the only final calibration basis; other viewers' opinions are misread clues only, not calibration. Records use fixed columns; family films record no identifiable personal detail. (D-M185)
69. Product films get one stranger-audience test before delivery: the viewer took no part in the project, is not on the client team, has not seen the script and roughly matches the target audience; one viewing, no prompting, a fixed set of three questions (what the product is and who it is for; which segments they believed and did not believe, and why; what they want to do after watching — this three-question set is the only version). Answers are recorded verbatim and organised into a misread list; liking or scores never substitute. (D-M174)
70. Product-film test headcount starts at one: if a core message is misread, first check it against the model blind-test list and pinpoint the shot; only when no shot can be pinpointed and it looks accidental do you add a second, new viewer — never re-show the same person. One pass is not proof of persuasiveness. A fixed 3–5 viewers is not adopted. About five minutes is an Automation Video instance, untested. (D-M174)
71. Private use does not trigger social-upload restrictions: the family series and the birthday film are private and are not bound by social-upload limits. (D-M170)
72. Before any film goes public, recheck the current licence terms first: BBC, Mixkit and third-party IP inside the film (for example Brawl Stars and Supercell in the birthday film), with sources, dates and unconfirmed points marked. (D-M170)
73. For family films the model blind-test question set is five questions plus story-comprehension and emotion questions (the birthday film's final version); it is a different stream from the product-film three-question set and the two are never mixed. (D-K20, D-048)

## 5.8 Failure modes

74. M37's cause is finalised as a pairing miss (a subclass of class 3, detection interference): the original program score_one.py scored only the single largest-bbox face, with no lead tracking, against the centroid of 5 main reference photos, so it scored the wrong face. (D-M211, D-M214, finalising D-M209)
75. M37 was classed by proxy measurement: frames from the 720p final cut with the child lead's reference set show the child detected in all 144 frames with likeness 0.26–0.57, cartoon frames at 0.02–0.16, the two groups not overlapping, and an ROI re-score median delta of +0.004 — a pairing miss under the three-cell decision table. Absolute scores are not compared against the original 0.097. Evidence grade: supported by proxy measurement, not proven on the original keyframes. The segment attribution was confirmed against the EDL (M37 starts at 174.2 s, M38 at 180.2 s). A per-frame re-run on the original keyframes is an optional tracking item, not a precondition for convergence. (D-M208, D-M207)
76. Lead-frame hard rule (expanded): for all real-person likeness QC including solo shots, designate the lead frame before reporting a lead score (position tracking or ROI designation); never let the largest face or the first returned frame stand for the lead, and write the frame-picking rule into the QC record. In multi-person frames, record every face frame; report the gap between the lead frame's score and the most-similar non-lead frame's score, and when the gap is small or negative, inspect the frames by eye (the gap is an inspection prompt, not a pass line). Record side-turn angle together with the score; angle-band figures are single-project instances, recomputed per film. (D-M211, D-M214)
77. Poor reference-photo quality lowering the likeness ceiling is its own failure mode: for a low score, check reference-photo and character-sheet quality first, not the individual shot or the generation result. (D-M181, D-M56)
