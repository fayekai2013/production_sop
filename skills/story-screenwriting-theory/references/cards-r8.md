#### Artefact: Bilingual camera glossary (glossary_camera_terms/)

- [1] Extreme long shot (ELS) / 大遠景 — The figure reads as subordinate to place and scale; distance and smallness are available for readings of insignificance or exposure, but the shot's settled function is orientation, not emotion. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [2] Long shot / wide shot (LS / WS) / 遠景（全景視野） — Balances figure against environment, so relations between character, others, and place stay readable; the default geography-establishing scale. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [3] Full shot (FS) / 全景 — The body as a whole becomes the information carrier: posture, gait, and action read before facial detail does. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [4] Medium long shot (MLS); American shot / cowboy shot / 中全景（美式鏡頭／牛仔鏡頭） — Keeps gesture, stance, and hand action legible while faces begin to matter; a working scale for confrontation and movement. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [5] Medium shot (MS) / 中景 — The conversational default: face, gesture, and some setting are simultaneously available, so no single channel is forced on the viewer. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [6] Medium close-up (MCU) / 中近景（近景） — Attention narrows toward the face and upper-body reaction while a residue of setting preserves spatial anchoring. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [7] Close-up (CU) / 特寫 — Larger, less cluttered faces are read for emotion faster in experimental tests, and faces draw gaze preferentially; the further claim that a close-up by itself produces empathy is a theoretical position (Balazs, Plantinga), not an experimental result. — grade A — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [8] Extreme close-up (ECU) / 大特寫 — Forces attention onto a single detail and removes contextual competition; readings of intensity or shock are context-dependent craft lore. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [9] Establishing shot / 建立鏡頭（定場鏡頭） — Builds the viewer's spatial model early, which later closer framings can then spend; a comprehension function supported indirectly by the continuity and attention literature. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [10] Two-shot / 雙人鏡頭 — Keeps both parties in one attentional field, so relation, reaction, and power geometry are judged simultaneously rather than assembled across cuts. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [11] Over-the-shoulder shot (OTS) / 過肩鏡頭 — Anchors the viewer spatially and socially inside the exchange: we attend with one party while reading the other; depends on consistent eyelines and the axis (Section 6). — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [12] Point-of-view shot (POV shot) / 主觀鏡頭（視點鏡頭） — Gaze cues are among the strongest natural attention cues, and the bracketed structure invites the viewer to take the look as the character's; full identification with the character does not follow automatically and is not claimed here. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [13] Insert / 插入鏡頭（特寫插入） — A pure attention instruction: it guarantees registration of a narrative fact. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [14] Cutaway / 切出鏡頭（旁切鏡頭） — Relieves or redirects attention and can carry reaction, environment, or parallel information; any emotional colouring comes from the chosen content, not the device. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [15] Aerial shot / 空拍鏡頭（鳥瞰鏡頭，當其為正俯時） — Height and detachment make pattern and scale legible and persons small; the common gloss of an omniscient or godlike viewpoint is craft lore. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [16] Eye-level angle / 平角度（平視鏡頭） — Treated in craft tradition as the neutral baseline against which raised and lowered angles are read; neutrality is itself a convention, since framing, lens, and distance still act on the viewer. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [17] High angle / 俯角（俯拍） — The textbook claim (subject looks smaller, weaker, more vulnerable) has real but mixed experimental support: vertical position is a genuine perceptual symbol of power, yet angle studies disagree in direction and size by context, and one 2023 videoconference study partly reversed the expected direction. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [18] Low angle / 仰角（仰拍） — The mirror claim (subject looks larger, more powerful, more dominant) shares the same evidence status as the high angle: supported as a verticality metaphor and in some photo and video studies, inconsistent across settings. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [19] Dutch angle (canted angle) / 荷蘭式傾斜鏡頭（斜角鏡頭） — Craft lore associates the broken horizontal with unease, instability, or a disturbed world; no direct experimental test of the filmic device was found in this pass, so the claim stays at lore level. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [20] Overhead shot / top shot (bird's-eye) / 正俯鏡頭（鳥瞰鏡頭） — Flattens persons into pattern and diagram; experimental photo work finds figures from an extreme top view rated weaker and more passive, alongside some ratings in the opposite, composed and competent direction, so no single feeling may be asserted. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [21] Worm's-eye view / 蟲視角（極低仰角） — Exaggerates height and monumentality by perspective geometry; glosses such as menace or awe are craft lore built on that geometry. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [22] Static shot / 固定鏡頭（定鏡） — Stillness transfers all motion, and therefore attention, to what moves inside the frame; readings of detachment, patience, or entrapment are directorial uses, graded as lore. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [23] Pan / 搖鏡頭（水平搖攝，pan） — Mimics a turning head, so it reads as looking along a space and can connect two points into one spatial statement; speed changes its energy, not its basic scanning function. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [24] Tilt / 俯仰搖鏡（tilt） — Mimics raising or lowering the gaze; a tilt up a figure or building discloses scale in time rather than all at once. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [25] Dolly shot (dolly in / dolly out) / 軌道推軌鏡頭（推軌／拉軌） — Physical approach changes relative sizes and parallax, which is why a dolly in feels like approach rather than magnification (contrast zoom, below); the emotional gloss of growing intimacy or abandonment is a use, not a property. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [26] Tracking shot / trucking shot / 跟拍鏡頭（移動跟攝） — Sustained co-movement holds the subject at the centre of attention while the world flows past; claims that it induces dreamlike or trance states at slow speeds are craft lore. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [27] Crane shot / boom shot / 升降鏡頭（搖臂鏡頭） — Large vertical revelation of space; the traditional gloss of an omniscient or liberated viewpoint is craft lore, and the same move can equally read as abandonment of the figures below. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [28] Arc shot / 弧形環繞鏡頭 — Rotating background parallax keeps recontextualising a held subject, which is why it is used for charged stillness in dialogue or confrontation; a fixed emotional meaning is lore. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [29] Handheld shot / 手持鏡頭 — Craft and scholarly tradition both associate the instability with immediacy, documentary presence, and bodily involvement; the association is a learned convention reinforced by news and documentary usage, not a measured reflex. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [30] Steadicam / gimbal shot / 穩定器鏡頭（Steadicam／三軸穩定器） — Smooth floating travel sustains spatial continuity inside long takes and keeps attention unbroken; glosses of dreamlike glide or predatory pursuit are use-dependent lore. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [31] Zoom shot / 變焦鏡頭（zoom） — Magnification without approach: sizes scale together and no new parallax appears, which is the perceptual basis for calling a zoom flatter and less bodily than a dolly; evaluation of that flatness is craft judgement. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [32] Dolly zoom (Vertigo effect) / 推軌變焦（希區考克變焦／眩暈效果） — The one movement whose unsettling effect has a stated perceptual mechanism: perspective changes while subject size does not, a combination normal vision never produces (see RN-07). — grade A — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [33] Whip pan / 甩搖鏡頭（快搖） — The blur wipes one view into another and injects kinetic energy; used as a transition or an excitement device by convention. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [34] Roll / 滾轉鏡頭（roll） — Tilts the whole world's vertical; stronger and rarer than a static Dutch angle, and like it supported only by craft lore about disorientation. — grade C — flags: SINGLE_SOURCE — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [35] Focal length / 焦距（焦段） — Focal length alone does not change perspective; camera position does. — grade A — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [36] Wide-angle lens (short focal length) / 廣角鏡頭 — Primary picture-perception research: short-lens pictures look stretched in depth and faces look rounder and less flat, because viewers do not view the picture from its centre of projection; the further gloss of aggression or comic distortion in close-up is use, not finding. — grade A — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [37] Normal / standard lens / 標準鏡頭 — The naturalness of the standard lens is explained, not just asserted, by the viewing-distance account: it maximises the chance the viewer sits near the correct centre of projection. — grade A — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [38] Telephoto lens (long focal length) / 望遠鏡頭（長焦鏡頭） — Primary research supports depth compression and flatter faces, with reported social readings (for example a face judged more distant or less approachable) resting on thinner, single-study ground; crowd and pursuit compression effects are reliable geometry, their tension is interpretation. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [39] Deep focus (deep depth of field) / 深焦（大景深） — Leaves attention unforced: several planes compete, so the viewer must search; that openness is the factual basis under the Bazinian claim that deep focus respects ambiguity and viewer freedom, which itself is a theoretical position, graded as such. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [40] Shallow focus (shallow depth of field) / 淺焦（淺景深） — Sharpness is a strong attention cue and blur removes competitors, so shallow focus reliably narrows where viewers look first; the further gloss of intimacy or subjectivity is interpretation layered on that guidance. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [41] Rack focus (focus pull) / 跟焦轉移（移焦，rack focus） — A timed attention hand-over inside the shot: the newly sharp plane captures gaze without a cut, so the device can link, reveal, or demote subjects in a chosen order. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [42] Split diopter / 分屈光鏡（裂像屈光鏡，split diopter） — Sustains two distant centres of attention in one image and makes the viewer hold an impossible double focus; readings of divided loyalty or surveillance are interpretive uses. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [43] Tilt-shift / 移軸鏡頭 — The miniature or toy-world reading of tilted focus is a perceptual inference from an unnaturally thin sharp band; in narrative film the device is rare and its meaning is not conventionalised. — grade C — flags: SINGLE_SOURCE — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [44] Key light / 主光 — Direction and hardness of the key are the largest single lever on how a face reads; specific mood claims belong to the light package. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [45] Fill light / 補光（輔光） — Fill level controls how much of the shadow side stays legible, and with it how open or withheld a face feels; ratio claims in depth belong to the light package. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [46] Back light / 背光（逆光） — Its settled function is separation and depth on a two-dimensional image (the ASC teaching formulation: shadow, separation, fill); halo and silhouette readings follow from exposure choices. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [47] Rim light / 輪廓光（邊緣光） — Makes the figure's boundary pop from its ground, a figure-ground effect; glamour or spectral glosses are uses, not properties. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [48] High-key lighting / 高調照明 — Craft tradition pairs it with openness and comedy or musical registers; the pairing is genre convention, not a measured effect. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [49] Low-key lighting / 低調照明 — Craft tradition pairs it with concealment and noir or thriller registers; the same caution applies: convention first, effect unproven in isolation. — grade C — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [50] Practical light / 實景光源（畫面內光源） — A visible source makes the lighting feel caused inside the world, supporting belief in the space; it is also the usual anchor for motivated light. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [51] Motivated light / 有動機光（動機光） — Plausible causation keeps lighting from drawing attention to itself, which is a credibility claim about the viewer's acceptance, not a mood claim. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [52] Blocking / 走位調度（場面調度之演員調度） — Blocking is the pre-camera allocation of attention and power geometry: who is near, high, lit, and moving is decided here before any lens choice acts on it. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [53] Crossing / 橫越畫面（穿越走位） — A moving figure captures attention against static surroundings, and a crossing re-draws the scene's power geometry in time rather than by cut. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [54] Staging in depth / 縱深調度（深度調度） — Holds competing centres of action in one shot and lets attention travel in depth; it is the staging partner of deep focus (Section 4). — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [55] Planimetric composition / 平面式構圖（正面平面調度） — Flatness suppresses depth travel and makes the frame read as a tableau or diagram; readings of detachment or deadpan are filmmaker uses of that flatness. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [56] Axis of action / 180-degree line / 動作軸線（180 度線） — Empirical work is narrower than the craft rule: crossing the line measurably worsens viewers' spatial representation and object-location memory, while narrative comprehension and event order survive; the rule protects geography, not story understanding. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [57] Eyeline (eyeline match) / 視線（視線銜接） — Another person's gaze direction is a reflexive attention cue for viewers, which is the perceptual engine under eyeline matching, POV bracketing, and shot and reverse-shot construction. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
- [58] Screen direction / 銀幕方向（畫面方向） — Preserves the viewer's running spatial model so movement reads as continuing rather than reversing; it is the motion-side consequence of the axis rule, with the same evidence limits. — grade B — flags: none — <story-theory-base>/theory/glossary_camera_terms/glossary_en_zh.md
